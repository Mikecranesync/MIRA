**Subject:** Article pitch (exclusive): Where a small maintenance crew's know-how lives when the senior tech retires

Hi [Editor name],

I'd like to pitch an exclusive, noncommercial feature for Plant Engineering.

**Working title:** Where your crew's know-how lives when the senior tech retires: a practical guide to manual knowledge for small maintenance teams

**Abstract (about 100 words):**
In a plant with a 2–15 person maintenance crew, the fix for most breakdowns is written down somewhere: in an OEM manual, a binder, or one veteran's head. When that veteran leaves, the crew loses the map to the manuals as much as the knowledge itself. This article draws on conversations with maintenance managers at small and mid-size plants to show where troubleshooting time actually goes, and gives a practical, tool-agnostic checklist for organizing manuals, fault tables and tribal knowledge so any tech on any shift can find the right page fast.

**Outline:**
1. The 2 a.m. problem: a real (anonymized) breakdown story from a small plant.
2. Where the answer usually was: manual, OEM support, forums, the veteran tech.
3. Why mixed-brand equipment makes it harder (Rockwell, Siemens, Mitsubishi on one line).
4. A practical checklist: inventory the machines, collect the right manual revisions, mark the fault-table and wiring pages, decide where manuals live, capture the veteran's notes before they leave.
5. Where AI tools fit, and where they don't: why any answer, human or software, should point to the page it came from.
6. A 30-day plan any crew can start Monday.

**About me:** Mike Harper, founder of FactoryLM (Central Florida), which builds a tool that answers maintenance questions from a plant's own manuals with page citations. The article doesn't promote it; the bio line is the only mention.

**Length:** about 1,500–2,000 words, delivered as a Word file within 3 weeks of your go-ahead.
