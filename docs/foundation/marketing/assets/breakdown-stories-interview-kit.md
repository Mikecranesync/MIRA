# "Breakdown Stories" interview kit
*Tactic T27. Mike sends every invite himself, one at a time. This is a personal invitation to share expertise, not a sales email, and it stays within the cold-email hold: no sequences, one polite nudge max. Bots draft; Mike sends, records and publishes.*

## Who to invite (priority order)
1. People Mike already knows who run or work in plant maintenance.
2. People who comment on or DM about Mike's LinkedIn clips.
3. Maintenance managers / lead techs at 50–500 person plants in Florida and the Southeast with mixed AB/Siemens/Mitsubishi equipment (Pipeline Scout drafts names from public LinkedIn profiles and company sites; no scraping tools).
4. People met at SMRP/ISA meetings (ask in person).

## Invite (LinkedIn message or email, personalize the first line)
> Hi [First name], [real, specific reason: "your comment on downtime at 3 a.m. stuck with me" / "we met at the SMRP meeting"].
>
> I'm recording short conversations with maintenance managers about one thing: your worst breakdown, and how your crew found the answer. It's 20 minutes on video. No pitch, and nothing to buy. I'm collecting real stories about how small crews keep mixed equipment running.
>
> If I publish anything, it's a 60–90 second clip, and only after you've seen it and said OK. I can leave your plant's name out.
>
> Would [two specific time options] work?
>
> Mike Harper, founder, FactoryLM

**One nudge (after 5 business days, only if no reply):** "No worries if the timing's bad. The offer stands whenever things calm down." Then stop.

## The 20-minute run sheet
1. (1 min) Thanks; confirm recording is OK; remind them they approve any clip.
2. **Tell me about your plant and crew.** How many people, what equipment brands, how old?
3. **Your worst breakdown in the last year or two.** What went down, what time, what did it cost you in hours?
4. **How did you find the answer?** Manual, OEM phone line, a forum, YouTube, the old-timer, ChatGPT?
5. **Where do your manuals live today?** Binder, shared drive, someone's laptop, the OEM site?
6. **What happens when your most experienced tech retires or leaves?**
7. **If you could fix one thing about troubleshooting on your floor, what would it be?**
8. (1 min) Thanks. "Can I send you the clip to approve?"

**After the recording only, and only if they ask** "what do you build?": show a 5-minute live look on one of their machines' manuals. Never pitch inside the episode.

## Permission note (send with the draft clip)
> Here's the clip I'd like to post. If you're OK with it as is, just reply "approved." If you'd like your name, your plant or any detail removed, tell me and I'll change it or drop it.

Keep the written approval (email/DM) in HubSpot with the contact record.

## Publishing format
- 60–90 s vertical clip with captions; one story beat (the breakdown + how they found the fix).
- Text post: 3–5 lines, the guest's lesson in their own words, credit them (if they agreed), and end with a question to other maintenance folks.
- No product mention in the post. Mike's profile does that job.

## Bot roles
- **Pipeline Scout:** candidate guest list (30 to start), with one specific, true personalization line each.
- **Field Voice:** personalized invite drafts, the clip caption and text post, quote pulls for the trade article.
- **Marketing Lead:** tracks invites → recorded → approved → published in the Friday scorecard.

## Guardrails
- Real stories only, never composite or invented ones.
- No safety-procedure advice in clips (lockout, arc flash, confined space): cut those parts.
- No plant names, logos or numbers without written OK.
- Invite only people Mike has a genuine reason to contact; no bulk messaging, no automation tools on LinkedIn.
