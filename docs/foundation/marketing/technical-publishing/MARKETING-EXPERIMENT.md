# First marketing experiment: useful drive references that improve the product

**Status:** proposed four-week pilot; prepared 2026-10-25 (America/New_York). This document defines the study and prepares copy. It records no live launch, outreach, advertising, audience results or product test. Begin the four weeks when the first public release is authorized under the existing publication process, not on the date this file was written.

## Recommendation and question

Start with one source-checked PowerFlex 525 F004/F005 article, its downloadable two-page PDF and a small number of conversations with relevant technicians. Use the approved sample in [examples/powerflex-525](examples/powerflex-525/) as the design reference. The early question is:

**Can a technician use this explanation to identify the evidence needed next, and can what we learn improve the article, MIRA or the FactoryLM experience?**

The commercial hypothesis is that useful, credible maintenance references can introduce FactoryLM to relevant people and lead to voluntary product conversations. That is a hypothesis to test. A polished document demonstrates editorial capability; it does not demonstrate that MIRA can diagnose the case, that a reader repaired a machine or that the audience wants a product.

The existing [learning pilot](../style-guides/BLOG-PLAN.md) remains the product-learning plan. This marketing experiment supplies distribution and feedback around that plan. Keep the first cycle small enough to inspect every material reader observation and every proposed product finding.

## Audience, offer and destination

Treat these audience roles as recruiting hypotheses and record which roles actually respond:

| Reader | Immediate job | Offer to test |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance technician working with drives | Preserve useful diagnostic information and avoid jumping from a code to an unsupported cause. | A readable article and printable evidence reference. |
| Controls engineer or experienced troubleshooter | Check the interpretation, source applicability and information needed for escalation. | Exact OEM references and a concise place to report corrections or missing context. |
| Maintenance lead | Improve the quality of the handoff between shifts or to specialists. | A repeatable evidence summary and an optional conversation about the existing workflow. |

Publish the complete explanation as accessible HTML with a direct PDF download. Keep both open for this first experiment. Requiring contact details before reading would introduce another variable before we know whether the reference is useful. A reader may voluntarily offer feedback or ask about the product after receiving value. Do not count a download as a lead.

The proposed destination needs the exact equipment/problem title, one clear summary, the full source-backed content, edition/review information, PDF download, correction route and one primary feedback request. Include a byline or accountable organization label consistent with the style guide. Disclose AI assistance and distinguish source review from equipment testing. Reuse the source ledger for claims; do not add unsupported speed, diagnosis or repair claims in headings, previews, image text or calls to action.

Use the generated cover as an editorial illustration. Keep the small illustration label legible and preserve its meaning in alt text. It is not a device photograph, measured fault trace or MIRA screenshot. Prepare a separate social preview only when needed; inspect the actual preview rather than cropping important title text out of the approved cover.

### Primary call to action

> Was this enough to identify what you would record next? Tell us what is missing or unclear.

The secondary invitation can ask whether the reader wants to discuss how their team finds and hands off maintenance information. Product demonstrations require their own current evidence; the article is not a substitute for that evidence.

## Four-week plan

The sequence is relative to the authorized launch. Owners are roles to assign for the cycle; this file does not schedule work or contact anyone.

| Week | Work and owner | Evidence to retain | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — prepare and observe** | Editorial owner completes the source ledger, technical review and visual review. Publication owner releases the approved HTML/PDF under existing authority. Analyst verifies measurement and records baseline availability. Mike or a designated relationship owner invites a small, relevant set of existing contacts to try the reading task. | Exact released revision and hashes; judge reports; destination and download behavior; measurement test excluded from audience counts; invite/response counts and recruitment context. | Is the release usable, and do readers understand the intended next evidence? Fix substantive confusion before wider distribution. |
| **2 — discuss one useful point** | Publication owner shares one substantive LinkedIn post from an appropriate owned account, with the article link. Relationship owner follows up only where appropriate. Analyst groups feedback. | Published copy/URL/date, campaign tag, platform-native results if available, destination visits, verbatim or faithfully summarized observations with permission for reuse. | Does the post bring relevant readers or questions? Separate weak distribution from weak content. |
| **3 — revise and test transfer** | Editorial owner addresses supported feedback and reruns affected reviews. Product owner normalizes one or more relevant questions for the existing learning pilot. A second LinkedIn document post is optional if the first discussion justifies testing that format. | Revision diff; changed review bindings; product case IDs and actual run references where available; separate format/channel records. | Is there a demonstrated content issue, product gap, source gap or merely a new hypothesis? Does the revised document improve the specific task? |
| **4 — decide what to repeat** | Analyst and editorial/product owners review counts, observations, effort and unresolved items. Select the next narrow topic only if the cycle was useful and feasible. | One short readout with denominators, recruitment limits, costs in time/tool usage, open questions and next decision. | Continue the same topic/channel, revise the offer, test a different problem or pause expansion. |

For planning, aim to obtain **five completed reading-task observations** across at least two relevant roles. This is a small learning-sample goal chosen for the pilot, not a performance benchmark or a statistically representative sample. Record fewer observations honestly if recruitment is limited. One consequential error is enough to revise the affected content; a positive majority does not cancel it.

Do not treat two LinkedIn posts in different weeks as a controlled A/B test. Timing, audience and distribution differ. Use them as descriptive observations unless a later experiment can control those factors. Do not expand to paid acquisition until usefulness, destination behavior and inquiry handling are understood well enough to state what a paid test would learn.

## Measurement without inflated claims

Start with existing approved site analytics, server/download data, platform-native reporting and the editorial record. Identify the actual available tool and owner before launch. This package does not install tracking or an analytics service. If an event is unavailable, label it **unmeasured**; do not estimate it from an unrelated number.

| Measure | Definition and denominator | Interpretation and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Reading-task usefulness | Readers who can identify the next evidence or decision supported by the article / readers who attempted the task. Retain the actual response and recruitment method. | Primary learning measure. A generic positive reaction is weaker evidence; no result establishes a field repair. |
| Relevant audience feedback | Distinct substantive observations from people with relevant roles/context, grouped by issue. | Count people and observations separately; one person may report several issues. Record relationship bias. |
| Article discovery | Available visits or sessions to the article, segmented by recorded source; search impressions/clicks separately if Search Console is available. | Show definitions, observation window and missing data. Platform impressions are not site visits. |
| PDF interest | Verified download clicks or requests, using the actual instrumented definition, alongside article sessions when meaningful. | Clicks, completed transfers, unique readers and offline sharing differ. Do not call them interchangeable. |
| Qualified product inquiries | Distinct real inquiries expressing a relevant maintenance workflow and interest in discussing FactoryLM. | Keep feedback, inquiries, meetings and agreed pilots separate. Report counts first; show unknown attribution. |
| Learning converted into verified work | Unique supported content corrections; valid product cases; reproduced product defects; repairs with comparable retest. | Do not count one issue several times because it appeared in several questions. A proposed fix is not a verified improvement. |
| Production effort | Human review minutes and available tool usage per released revision, including failed attempts. | Helps decide what to automate. Record actual effort; no unsupported efficiency claim. |

Use a stable campaign label such as `pf525-evidence-pilot` and distinguish link-post and document-post content tags when the destination analytics supports them. Use campaign tags without personal data. Preserve the untagged canonical URL. Record known delivery channel plus reader self-report when available; forwarded PDFs, copied links, cookie restrictions and offline sharing leave attribution gaps. Do not force every inquiry into a channel.

Report small samples as counts, for example **“three of five participants identified the intended evidence; two misunderstood the snapshot limit.”** This is an example of reporting form, not a result. Show the actual question asked, revision tested and who was recruited. No traffic or conversion baseline has been collected for this pilot.

### Reading-task and feedback record

Ask readers to use a desk scenario; this experiment does not ask them to operate or measure equipment:

1. “After reading this, what information would you record before deciding what caused F004/F005?”
2. “What can the stored fault/bus-voltage pairing tell you, and what does it leave uncertain?”
3. “Which sentence, table or source link would you change or need explained?”
4. “Where would you use this: during a handoff, when reviewing a fault, or somewhere else?”

The task checks comprehension of the approved content. Retain role/context, article revision, response, ambiguity, follow-up question and whether the observation can be quoted or reused. Record product interest separately and let respondents decline it. Avoid collecting plant identifiers or private operational material when a normalized question is enough.

## Turning marketing observations into MIRA and FactoryLM improvements

Use a compact chain: **question → reviewed sources → document → reader observation → classified finding → applicable test/change → verification**. Keep links in the existing editorial record and Answer Radar records described in [BLOG-PLAN](../style-guides/BLOG-PLAN.md); do not create a second product case database.

| Observation | Initial classification | Evidence needed next |
|---|---|---|
| Readers pair F651 with the wrong historical fault after reading the table. | Content comprehension issue. | The exact version and reader response; revise the explanation, review affected claims and retest the task. |
| A reader asks whether MIRA can interpret the same evidence. | Product evaluation opportunity. | Freeze a normalized question and applicable source identity, then observe the actual product under a named condition/build. |
| A valid product run invents a confirmed cause or cites an unrelated manual. | Suspected product defect. | Retain answer, citation passages, setup and reference assertions; adjudicate, deduplicate and reproduce as appropriate before proposing repair. |
| A reader cannot find the download or source links. | Publishing/usability finding. | Observe the actual destination/device behavior; distinguish site integration from the renderer and fix the demonstrated issue. |
| Several people request the same missing equipment scope. | Topic or source-coverage hypothesis. | Confirm the missing scope and whether applicable sources exist; retain negative results and do not invent answers to satisfy demand. |
| A reader reports a repaired machine. | Reader-reported outcome. | Preserve attribution and context; it is not independently verified, caused by the article, or proof of MIRA performance. |

The existing Answer Radar components are reusable. The reviewed-case adapter and complete article-to-run integration remain proposed unless a separate implementation/run supplies evidence. This publishing workflow must not report that an article or judge score executed a product test.

Keep these familiar cases in development. Once the draft, sources or expected answers inform a repair, nearby paraphrases are not independent fresh evaluation. Compare repairs using the same frozen question, source edition and named condition, and report staging retest, release and production observation separately.

## End-of-pilot decision record

Produce a short readout answering:

- What was released, where and under which revision?
- Who actually read or tested it, and what could they do afterward?
- Which distribution observations are measured, and which are unknown?
- Which content corrections, source gaps, product evaluations and verified improvements resulted?
- Did relevant people voluntarily request a product conversation?
- How much authoring and review effort did the cycle take?
- What specific change or next experiment is justified?

Continue when the document helps the intended task and the process produces useful evidence at manageable effort. Revise when specific misunderstandings recur. If reach is low, improve access to relevant readers before declaring the topic ineffective. If content is useful but no one asks about the product, investigate the product offer separately. An honest result can be “useful reference, commercial hypothesis still unproven.”
