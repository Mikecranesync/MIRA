**November 1, 2026. Documentation and proposed acceptance criteria; not release approval or proof of customer demand.**

## First-use promise

For the explicitly supported assets, manuals and jobs: **ask about the fault, inspect the supporting passage, get a safe and useful next step, and leave reliable evidence for the next technician.**

Prepare the customer's relevant manuals before the pilot, confirm applicability and make readiness visible. Start with one qualified equipment family/job; expand coverage one accepted manual pack at a time. Before a broad multi-OEM offer, qualify three named packs from the actual pilot inventory. The map's suggested twenty-manual collection is an onboarding hypothesis, not accepted coverage or a reason to index unrelated documents during an outage.

Keep manual upload/selection available when assisted identification is unqualified. Nameplate vision, offline reasoning and CMMS integration are separately accepted extensions. Preserve the current Mission 001 work and make its confirmed observation-to-state loop feed the useful-answer path.

## How the vision helps today's urgent job

1. A prepared, applicable knowledge base shortens the hunt for the right passage.
2. Confirmed technician observations tell MIRA what is known about this particular asset now.
3. MIRA combines those two sources, preserves safety conditions and exposes uncertainty.
4. A sourced packet or guided branch helps the technician act under the site's procedures.
5. Recorded outcome evidence helps the next technician avoid starting from zero.
6. Pilot receipts determine whether this actually saves effort and earns voluntary use.

The Level 3 plan remains the complete product target. A source-qualified answer can deliver initial relief before every extension exists, but an incomplete pilot must not be labeled the complete Level 3 package. Any promised first-use feature retains its own acceptance gate.
