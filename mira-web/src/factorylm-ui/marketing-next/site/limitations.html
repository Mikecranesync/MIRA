What MIRA does, and what it doesn’t

A plain account of where MIRA helps, where it stops, and how to use it safely.

What MIRA does

    Answers from the documents you attachIn an equipment notebook, ask about your OEM manuals or readable PDF electrical prints, with a source page to check.
  • ✓
    Says when the manual doesn't cover itIf the attached manual doesn't answer the question, MIRA says it can't find it there instead of guessing.

What MIRA doesn't do

  • It isn't a CMMS and doesn't replace yours. There's nothing to switch. CMMS connections are optional add-ons.
  • It doesn't read PLC data or write work orders to your CMMS.
  • It doesn't replace qualified maintenance personnel, your safety program or the manufacturer's procedures.

Electrical prints and drawing sets

Attach electrical prints as a PDF to the equipment notebook. Ask about the component references, device schedules and drawing notes available as readable text. A cited answer points you back to the document so you can check the original sheet.

Searchable text matters. A scan or a drawing made mainly of symbols and lines may contain too little text to answer a question. Uploading a file alone does not prove every sheet is searchable. Check the drawing revision and sheet against the equipment.

Complete circuit tracing and reliable interpretation of every symbol, wire number or terminal are not established capabilities. Verify circuit connections on the original prints and with qualified personnel.

Namespace building and plant integrations

MIRA’s namespace-building approach connects equipment context, evidence and human-reviewed relationships to support digital transformation. The self-service starting point is an equipment notebook with document questions. A complete plant namespace, live PLC/Ignition context and work-order integrations require a separately scoped rollout and confirmation of available connections. A conversation or saved note alone does not become approved diagnostic knowledge.

What we've tested

In production we've tested Rockwell, Festo and ABB manuals so far, in earlier production tests. This is not a current-build certification. Opening the cited page from a phone is still being improved; on a phone, check the page on desktop when it matters.

An earlier staging test answered a PLC-identification question from CV-101's nine-sheet electrical print set and cited page 1. That demonstrates one documented lookup, not coverage of the whole drawing set or current-build acceptance.

Safety

Disconnect power per the equipment manual and lock out per site procedure.

MIRA's answers are suggestions grounded in your equipment documents, not directives. Always check the cited page before acting, and follow your site's lockout/tagout, arc flash and confined-space procedures. Don't rely on any AI tool for those.
